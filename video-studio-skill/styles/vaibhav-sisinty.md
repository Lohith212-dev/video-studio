# Vaibhav Sisinty AI Update

## 1. The idea in one paragraph
Vaibhav Sisinty (about 877K YouTube subscribers; founder of the GrowthSchool learning platform and the "Staying Ahead" AI community) runs a weekly "here's what just happened in AI and how you can use it" show. The feeling is momentum: an upbeat host speaks directly to you from a cosy, cinematically lit studio (bookshelf, lamp, art on a dark wall, podcast mic), and every few seconds the edit rewards you with something new: a guest's name typed huge behind them, a number that ticks up, a news headline being highlighted, a terminal racing another terminal, a chart where one bar is orange. It looks premium (shallow depth of field, editorial typography with tight Inter-style sans and boxed labels) but it is built for retention and action: segment counters ("03/12"), "jump to the next update" permission, and a recurring call to join his WhatsApp community. It is aimed at Indian professionals, founders and students who want to stay ahead of AI without reading everything themselves.

## 2. Narration
- **Voice:** Male, early-30s feel, Indian-English accent, bright and confident, a little breathless with excitement, smiling while talking. Direct second person. Emphasis on superlatives and numbers ("100 times faster and 100 times cheaper").
- **Pace:** Measured from auto-subtitles. Long-form ECshcTsXSFs (first 6 min): 1,086 words, **181 wpm**, no pauses longer than 1.5 s. Short uoO2EIJoE58: 266 words in 74.5 s = **214 wpm**. Target 180-190 for 16:9, 205-215 for vertical.
- **Sentence shape:** 10-20 words, simple present tense, news verbs ("just dropped", "is finally here", "is blowing up"). Teasers and cliffhangers ("you won't believe its results", "I can't wait to show you what it does", "But here's where it gets wild"). Explains a tool by its benefit to "you" before its mechanism. Light jargon, always tied to a use.
- **Structure habits:** Cold open that previews the 3-4 biggest stories in 30-40 s with one visual each; "Those are just four of this week's 12 biggest AI updates"; then numbered segments, each opened with a chapter card (counter "03/12") and closed with "so what this means for you". Mid-roll CTA to the WhatsApp community. The Short follows hook (trending fact) -> what it does -> 2-3 real user examples -> "So here's the thing, you don't have to accept..." -> CTA.
- **HyperFrames TTS pick:** `am_michael` at speed 1.10 for 16:9 (about 180-185 wpm) and 1.20 for vertical; it is the most natural male voice in our list (Kokoro grade C+) and handles excited, plain sentences well. For a brighter, more "presenter" energy with a female voice, `af_nova` at 1.10 (grade C) or `af_heart` at 1.10 (grade A, warmer). None has an Indian accent. Add exclamation-free punctuation: Kokoro over-acts "!" at high speed.

## 4. Motion language
- **Signature moves:**
  1. **Text behind subject:** giant name or number set behind a cut-out of the person; letters type on left-to-right (0.04 s per character, `none`) or scale from 1.08 to 1 with fade (0.4 s, `power3.out`); a small label ("CO-Inventor of ChatGPT", "Cheaper") pops in its own outlined box (0.25 s, `back.out(1.6)`), connected by a leader line drawn with `stroke-dashoffset` (0.3 s, `power2.out`).
  2. **Boxed callout chain:** a word in a 2 px outline box ("AGI?") appears, a leader line elbows down/right from it (0.35 s, `power2.inOut`) to a dot, and the answer phrase types on next to the dot ("The answer was obviously not"). Then the camera slides left to the next phrase ("But" in an inverted black box with corner bracket marks, then "The trillion-dollar question is").
  3. **Pixel-block transition:** the outgoing shot breaks into a grid of large squares (about 160 px at 1920 wide, 12 columns) that flip to white/black in a random stagger (total 0.4-0.6 s, each cell 0.05 s, `steps(1)`), revealing the next scene. Use `grid-pixelate-wipe` from the registry.
  4. **Speed counter:** "13x" counts to "100x faster" (about 2 s, 0:06.0-0:08.0, `power2.out`, rounded integers, last value held 0.4 s) beside a stopwatch icon, with thin radial speed lines streaming outward from centre the whole time.
  5. **Chapter window:** the host A-roll scales from 1.0 to 0.72 (0.5 s, `power3.inOut`) inside a frame that gets a lime 2 px border with soft outer glow; below it a tick-mark ruler slides horizontally (1.2 s, `power2.inOut`) to the next chapter's notch, the outlined label swaps text, and the counter steps "02/12" -> "03/12"; then the window scales back to full frame or cuts to the segment's first visual. Total 2-3 s.
  6. **Highlighter on article:** yellow (`#FCFC70`) or lime (`#E4FC34`) rectangles sweep across 1-3 lines (`scaleX 0 to 1`, 0.4 s per line, `power2.out`, 0.1 s stagger) while the page drifts up slowly.
- **Transitions between scenes:** Mostly hard cuts on phrase boundaries; pixel-block wipes into big statements; quick whip/slide (0.25 s, `power3.inOut`) between consecutive white editorial panels; chapter window between segments.
- **Camera:** A-roll alternates a medium-wide framing with a tighter punch-in (a cut, not animated; e.g. 4:00 and 11:35 are tighter than 1:05 and 7:25), and the host gestures with both hands almost continuously. Graphics use slow drifts (scale 1.0 to 1.04 over the shot, `sine.inOut`) and a continuous diagonal pan while a callout chain grows (0:02.8-0:05.6).
- **Text reveals:** Typewriter for names and product names, including live edits ("Gr" -> "Grok 4" -> "Grok 4.7", 0:16.4-0:17.6); counters for multipliers, prices and counts ("13x" to "100x faster", "30+" to "40+ skills"); serif italic phrases fade-up 20 px (0.35 s, `power3.out`); exits by blurring out (8:16.4-8:17.6).

## 4b. Motion rules
1. **Carry one element across the cut.** The question "Are models that are superhuman at chat AGI?" is overlaid on the guest; the pixel-block wipe dissolves the guest and the boxed "AGI?" lands on the white callout page, while a pink pixel block shrinks into the leader line's dot (0:01.6-0:02.8).
2. **Numbers count, they never just appear.** "13x" to "100x faster" over about 2 s with radial speed lines streaming (0:06.0-0:08.0); a giant "30x" to "100x" counts behind the guest with a "Cheaper" box on a leader line (0:08.4-0:09.6); "30+" to "40+ skills, created by the community" while chip labels drift outward around it (8:10.0-8:11.2).
3. **Callout chains grow while the camera travels.** Box, elbow line, dot, then the answer typed word by word; the whole page pans diagonally as the inverted "But" box and "The trillion-dollar question is" arrive (0:02.8-0:05.6).
4. **Charts build, then decide.** Grey bars grow left to right with a stagger (0:18.0-0:19.2), hold half a second, and only then does the subject bar flip to orange (0:20.4).
5. **The host's frame moves between segments.** Chapter window: the A-roll shrinks into a lime-edged window (2:57.2), the window strip slides left like a film reel so the next segment's window enters from the right (2:58.4; 8:30.4-8:31.2, where a "Gemini Notebook" card slides in), the label retypes and the tick ruler scrolls; the window scales back to full frame at 3:02.0. About 5 s in total.
6. **Text pages are alive.** Loading dots morph into typed text ("Your data stays private. Zero data retention.", 8:22.0-8:25.6), then the dots orbit a privacy icon as the next card blurs in (8:26.0-8:26.8). Exits blur out rather than cut (8:16.4-8:17.6).
7. **B-roll runs in fast sequences.** Keynote and interview clips 1-2 s each, back to back (3:03.2-3:13.6), an article page sliding sideways mid-sequence (3:06.8-3:07.6).
8. **The host performs with his hands and face.** Constant two-hand gestures, raised eyebrows, open-mouth emphasis (2:50-2:57, 8:18-8:22); framing alternates medium-wide and tight between segments.

## 4d. Text rules
- **Captions:** long-form 16:9 has **none**. Vertical: Inter 600, 40-46 px, 3-5 words per group, on the violet box at the split-screen seam during the hook, then plain white with a soft shadow at about 70% height.
- **Callouts:** outlined boxes (2 px) with an elbow leader line and a dot; one question box plus one answer line, max 2 callouts per frame; answers type word by word.
- **Giant type:** one name or number, 300-420 px, behind a cut-out person, typed letter by letter or counted.
- **Text-only scenes: allowed, but never still.** White pages carry one short statement (8:22 "Your data stays private. Keep them under 4 s.

## 6. Data and numbers

## 7. Sound
- **Music:** Modern upbeat electronic / lo-fi tech beds, 100-125 BPM, steady energy, changes per segment; brief music-only stings under chapter cards. The Short's auto-subtitles tag "[music]" through its whole length; the long-form's subtitles never do, which suggests the long-form bed sits very low under the voice (or drops out in A-roll). An automated tempo estimate was inconclusive (varied 76-150 BPM across 30 s windows, low confidence), so treat BPM as a recommendation.
- **Sound effects:** Frequent but light: whoosh on chapter cards and slides, typing clicks for typewriter text, a soft "ding" when a counter lands, pixel "glitch" for mosaic wipes, pop for label boxes. About one SFX every 2-3 s in the cold open, sparser in segments.
- **Mix:** Voice at -14 LUFS; music at -22 to -26 dB under voice with 4-6 dB ducking; SFX at -16 to -20 dB (a little louder than in calmer styles).

## 10. HyperFrames build recipe
- **Canvas:** 1920x1080 at 30 fps for long-form style; 1080x1920 for the Short style (hook split 1080x960 top/bottom).
- **Layer order:** background (studio video / black / white page) -> giant background type -> person cut-out (from `npx hyperframes remove-background`) -> callout boxes and leader lines -> charts/screens -> captions -> transition overlay (pixel grid) on top.
- **Components to build once:**
  - `callout-box(text, inverted?)`: 2 px outline box, Inter Tight 500, with optional corner-bracket marks.
  - `leader-line(from, to, elbow)`: SVG path with dot terminal, drawn via dashoffset.
  - `chapter-window(label, index, total)`: scaled A-roll window with lime glow (`box-shadow: 0 0 24px rgba(228,252,52,.55)`), tick ruler (repeating-linear-gradient ticks 1 px every 8 px, taller ticks every 40 px), outlined label and mono counter.
  - `big-number(from, to, suffix, icon)` with radial speed lines (SVG lines with `strokeDashoffset` streak animation).
  - `bar-chart(rows, highlightIndex)` grey bars + one orange.
  - `cta-card(headline, highlightWord, phoneImage)` on peach gradient.
- **Registry blocks:** `grid-pixelate-wipe` (mosaic transition), `count-up` / `number-wheel` (counters), `animated-bar-chart` (restyle to grey + one orange), `mk-callout-highlight` and `hw-callout-circle` (callouts), `terminal-simulator` / `code-terminal-run` (terminal race), `x-post` (tweets), `browser-device-stage` (site demos), `caption-highlight` (vertical captions on a coloured box), `headline-slam` for news headlines.
- **Captions (vertical only):** groups of 3-5 words, Inter 600 44 px; during the split hook put them on the `#4C34C8` box at the seam (y about 960); afterwards plain white with shadow at y about 1350. Hard-kill each group.
- **Pitfalls:** the style is busy; keep each graphic simple (one idea, max 2 callouts). Do not use the lime everywhere; it marks navigation and highlights only. Do not let the pixel transition run more than 0.6 s. Text behind a person needs a good cut-out; check the 30-55% occlusion rule in the captions reference.

## 12. Do and don't
**Do**
- Preview the top 3-4 stories in the first 20-40 s with one striking visual each.
- Put a number or a person's name in giant type behind them.
- Show the real tool working (terminal, screen recording, output).
- Use one orange bar, one lime highlight, one pink name: one accent per frame.
- Number the segments and say how many there are.
- Close each segment with "what this means for you" and finish with a community/link CTA.

**Don't**
- Don't let any static visual sit longer than about 4 s.
- Don't use cartoon illustration or hand-drawn elements; the look is editorial-tech.
- Don't use heavy drop shadows, bevels or neon everywhere; the glow is reserved for the chapter window.
- Don't show charts without a single highlighted subject.
- Don't bury the hook; the first sentence carries the biggest claim.
- Don't use more than 5 words per caption group in vertical.

## 13. Feasibility with our stack
- **Reproduces well (85-90%):** callout chains, giant type, counters, pixel wipes, charts, chapter cards, article highlights, CTA cards, captions. All are HTML/SVG + GSAP and several have registry starting points.
- **Hard: the host and his studio.** The warm cinematic A-roll is a big part of the look and needs a person.
  1. **Faceless variant (free):** fill the host slot with warm-graded stock video of a person (section 4c) or, if the user accepts illustration, a posed Open Peeps host; keep the chapter window and the demo-corner picture-in-picture with that person in it. Text-behind-subject moves use cut-out press photos with parallax, or a product render or logo.
