# Aevy TV Explainer

## 1. The idea in one paragraph
Aevy TV (about 1.62M subscribers, started by Varun Mayya and Achina Mayya) is an Indian explainer channel that turns big public questions ("Why can't India build roads that last?", "Is North India stealing from South India?", "How China fixed its air") into 20-25 minute, well-sourced stories told by a relatable host. The channel's real look is a two-world edit. World one is a cinematic host set: a young presenter at a desk in a cosy study with dusty-pink walls, a lamp and a big window glowing with a lilac sunset, talking straight to camera with real emotion (frustration, disbelief). World two is a calm, almost museum-like cream canvas where all the evidence lives: news footage and photos placed as tidy cards, thin grey line charts that fill in a single wedge, rows of hollow squares that tick off one by one, measurement brackets with light italic numbers, and striking collage dioramas (a city cut-out sitting in a mountain bowl, layers of soil and sky) that make an abstract mechanism concrete. The contrast feels trustworthy and modern: it is serious journalism made for 18-35 year-old Indians who want to understand their country, with an Indian frame of reference ("our capital", rupees, Indian cities). The channel also runs a video-editing school ("learn to edit like AevyTV"), a sign that the motion design is the brand.

## 2. Narration
- **Voice:** In the reference, a female host in her mid-20s, Indian-English, warm and expressive, with real indignation on the key facts and a conversational "friend explaining" tone. The channel uses more than one host; the tone (direct, emotional, sceptical) is consistent.
- **Pace:** Measured from auto-subtitles of -VPYbJxPvzY (first 8 min): 1,346 words over 479 s = **169 wpm gross, 171 wpm net** (only 6 s of pauses longer than 1.5 s). Target 165-175 wpm with short beats after shocking numbers.
- **Sentence shape:** Short punchy sentences mixed with medium ones (6-18 words). Repeats a number for impact ("94 are in India. 94."). Colloquial intensifiers ("literally", "insane", "No, seriously", "And no, I'm not joking"). Direct address ("Right now, as you are watching this..."). Rhetorical questions that set up the next section ("Is this an unsolvable crisis? No, not really."). Teases ("Delhi's AQI hit an insane number... I'll tell you what it was in just a second"). Explains scales and units plainly (walks through the AQI bands 0-50, 51-100...).
- **Structure habits:** Cold open with a startling statistic and an Indian hook; a counter-example from another country ("Just look at the case of Beijing 2013"); the stakes for "us"; then a structured investigation (causes one by one, each with a mini-diagram), the comparison's solution, and a closing "so what should we do / accountability" segment. Sources are listed in the description; on screen, sources appear as real web pages and articles.
- **HyperFrames TTS pick:** `af_heart` at speed 1.05 (Kokoro grade A; warm, natural and able to carry emphasis). Male alternative: `am_michael` at 1.05. If a British-leaning, calmer read is wanted, `bf_emma` at 1.05 (grade B-). No approved voice has an Indian accent; keep Indian names and places but check pronunciation and respell phonetically in the script if Kokoro stumbles (e.g. "Dilli" vs "Delhi" is fine; test "Ghaziabad", "Noida").

## 3. Visual system
- **Palette** (sampled from frames of -VPYbJxPvzY; the cream canvas alone is 27% of all sampled pixels):
  - `#EAECE1` cream canvas (the evidence world; measured `#EAEBE3` to `#EAECE1`)
  - `#C9CAC1` hairline grey (frames, corner brackets, chart outlines, empty squares)
  - `#2B2B2B` ink (labels, headings)
  - `#292A2B` dark slate (alternate canvas for night/inversion scenes)
  - `#54A4EC` data blue (percent values, filled squares, PM10 particles; lighter tint `#8DB8E8` for fills)
  - `#EA8A5A` particle orange (PM2.5 particles, warm highlights)
  - `#D24A2A` sun red-orange (big disc/sun shapes in collages)
  - `#7EDE6E` signal green (one-off particle/marker accent)
  - Host set grade: dusty mauve walls `#7A4948` / `#623A37`, deep shadow `#372824`, lilac sunset window `#D8D9EB`, warm lamp highlight `#F4D9B0`.
- **Typography** (1920x1080). The channel uses a neutral neo-grotesk; Inter is the closest free match:
  - Section and chart titles: **Inter** 600, 30-36 px, `#2B2B2B`, sentence case, two short lines, top-left of a framed panel ("Factors of / death in a city").
  - Big concept words: **Inter** 800 caps, 40-48 px, tracking 0.01em ("GEOGRAPHICAL BOWL", "BEIJING").
  - Leader-line labels: **Inter** 700 caps 16-20 px ("YAN MOUNTAINS", "Warm air", "Cool air" in 400 sentence case 18 px).
  - Measurements and values: **Inter** 300 italic 40-56 px ("200m", "<2.5 µm"); big value "PM 2.5" in Inter 400 56-64 px; percent "15%" Inter 500 40 px in `#54A4EC`.
  - No burned-in subtitles. A tiny channel watermark sits top-right (about 40x14 px); use your own mark or nothing.
- **Backgrounds and texture:** Flat cream with no grain. Many scenes sit inside a thin-line rectangle inset about 32 px from the frame edge, with small corner brackets or dashed lines, like a museum display case. Dark slate canvas for night scenes. The host set is a real, softly lit room (shallow depth of field, warm practical lamp, sunset window).
- **Illustration / imagery:**
  - Real footage and photos (news clips, street scenes, hospitals, farms, satellite and Google Earth views) presented as **cards**: rectangles 30-45% of frame width with tiny 2-4 px corner radius, no border, no shadow, on cream; often two cards side by side or a stepped row.
  - **Collage dioramas:** photographic cut-outs layered into a scene: a block of terrain sliced like a cake (soil strata visible), a city skyline cut-out on top, mountains around it, a moon disc, wavy heat lines. These look like high-quality 3D/AI-assisted renders; for us they are built from PNG cut-outs.
  - **Thin-line diagrams:** 1.5 px grey outline pie charts, hollow square grids, measurement brackets, circles filled with particle dots.
  - Real web pages (ranking sites, news articles) screen-recorded with a blue text-selection highlight as if the viewer is reading along.
- **Layout:** 16:9, 32-64 px inset frames. Evidence scenes centre the subject with large empty cream space; text labels sit top-left or on leader lines. Two-up comparisons (Delhi | Beijing) place items left and right with labels under each. Never more than 2-3 images and 3 labels per frame.

## 4. Motion language
- **Signature moves:**
  1. **Fill-one-part chart:** a thin outline pie draws (0.6 s, `power2.inOut`), then a single wedge fills grey (`#C9CAC1` to 40% grey, 0.5 s, `power2.out`) and its value fades in beside it in blue; a side grid of hollow squares appears (stagger 0.03 s) and squares fill blue one by one (0.08 s each, `power1.out`) as the narrator counts.
  2. **Card step along a scale:** photo cards slide in from the right and settle in a row (0.5 s, `power3.out`, 0.15 s stagger) above a colour gradient bar (green to yellow to red to maroon) with tick values; the "camera" pans along the row as the value rises (2-4 s, `power2.inOut`), each new card hazier than the last.
  3. **Diorama flight and reveal:** the original's dioramas are 3D renders that the camera flies through (crane, sweep, orbit; 3:17-3:29). Our approximation: layered cut-outs that push 1.0 to 1.15 (4-6 s, `sine.inOut`) with parallax (foreground 1.2x, background 0.8x) plus a slow lateral pan, or a Three.js terrain (section 4c). Labels appear on leader lines that draw out from a point (0.4 s, `power2.out`) and type on.
  4. **Measurement bracket:** a vertical bracket draws from top to bottom beside an object (0.5 s, `power2.out`) and the italic value fades in ("1000m" then the object shrinks and the bracket re-draws "100m").
  5. **Particle circles:** circles of dots pop in (scale 0 to 1, 0.03 s stagger, `back.out(1.4)`) inside a hairline frame; dots of different colours mix to show composition (orange PM2.5, blue PM10, green CO).
  6. **Read-along screen recording:** a real web page scrolls; a blue selection highlight (`rgba(84,164,236,0.35)`) sweeps over the key line as if dragged by a mouse (0.4-0.6 s, `none`); a cursor may be visible.
- **Transitions between scenes:** Hard cuts between host and canvas. Within the canvas, one picture **becomes** the next instead of cutting: the soil-strata title card becomes the ground under a city, the city sinks away as heat lines rise out of it, the day canvas darkens to night as the sun sets and a moon rises (section 4b). Occasional fly-in into a satellite map. Transition durations 0.4-1.2 s when not a cut.
- **Camera:** Host locked off, but the edit alternates two angles every 3-5 s (a wide frontal shot and a closer three-quarter angle). Canvas scenes never stop moving: slow push-ins and drifts on charts, and continuous camera flights through 3D dioramas (crane up a mountain, pan down a valley, orbit a terrain cube). Satellite scenes fly in from region to city and tilt into 3D.
- **Text reveals:** Titles and panel headings fade (0.3-0.4 s, `power2.out`) or slide up 10-16 px. **Leader-line labels type on letter by letter** ("THE" then "THE HIMALAYAS", 3:18.0-3:18.4) and stack one by one; title text can delete itself word by word ("TEMPERATURE INVERSION" to "INVERSION" to nothing, 4:10.4-4:11.6). No kinetic slams. (Corrected 2026-10-30: the first audit said "no typewriter".)

## 4b. Motion rules
1. **The canvas always drifts.** After the pie builds, the whole panel keeps creeping (0:19.2-0:21.2); dioramas push and orbit continuously (3:08.8-3:10.8, 3:12.4-3:14.8). A finished picture is never frozen.
2. **Charts build in order: outline, part, value, then make room.** Pie circle draws clockwise and the title fades in word by word (0:17.2-0:17.6), one wedge fills (0:18.0), a leader line draws out (0:18.8), "15%" appears in blue (0:19.2); then the pie shrinks to the left while a column of hollow squares builds in from the right and the first one fills blue (0:21.6-0:23.2).
3. **One shape grows into the next.** A small blue tick stretches into the AQI gradient bar (0:31.6-0:32.4); photo cards then pop up from small to full size above it as the camera pans along the bar (0:32.8-0:35.6).
4. **Pictures transform instead of cutting.** Soil-strata title card becomes the ground under a city cut-out that rises into view, the sun disc slides in, a red heat layer fills the ground (4:10.0-4:14.4); the city sinks out of frame while wavy heat lines rise and wiggle in its place (4:15.2-4:18.8); the cream canvas darkens through grey to slate as the sun sinks, a moon appears and the city zooms up to fill the frame (4:19.2-4:21.2).
5. **Dioramas are camera flights, not static collages.** The camera cranes from the city up to the peak, labels "THE HIMALAYAS" (3:17.2-3:21.6), sweeps down the valley to "ARAVALLI HILLS" (3:22.0-3:24.0), pulls out to the whole terrain cube, then brown smog fills the bowl (3:24.4-3:28.8).
6. **Labels stack one per spoken line.** "Ground cools", "Cool air", "Warm air", "Cool air" arrive bottom to top on leader lines over 6 s while the ground layer glows and air bands fade in behind the city (4:22.0-4:28.0); then white smoke lines rise between the buildings (4:33.2).
7. **The host acts with her face and hands; the edit gives her two angles.** Pained, disbelieving expression held through a line (0:24.8-0:31.2); hands clasped, then opened and pointing (3:11.2, 3:16.0); wide frontal (3:06.8) and a closer three-quarter angle (3:11.2) alternate every 3-5 s.
8. **Maps fly.** The satellite view zooms continuously from city scale to streets and tilts into 3D with a place panel sliding in (0:13.2-0:16.8).

## 4d. Text rules
- **Captions: none.** No burned-in subtitles or kinetic captions.
- **Panel titles:** sentence case, two short lines, top-left of a framed panel, fade in word by word.
- **Callouts:** caps labels on thin leader lines that draw out from a point and type on letter by letter; stack them one per spoken line; at most 4 on screen.
- **Values:** one per panel, blue (`#54A4EC`) or light italic, next to the part it measures.
- **Text-only scenes: not allowed.** Even the "TEMPERATURE INVERSION" title sits on a photographic soil-strata texture that turns into the next scene. The only text-heavy frames are real web pages and articles, read along with a highlight.

## 6. Data and numbers
Restrained and visual rather than chart-heavy. One number per panel. Pies are outline-only with a single filled wedge; counts are grids of hollow squares that fill blue; ranges are gradient bars with photo cards; physical sizes use measurement brackets and particle circles; rankings are shown on the real website. Values in Inter Light italic or Inter Medium blue. A number stays on screen 3-6 s, longer than in the tech-news styles, and is often repeated by the host for emphasis.

## 7. Sound
- **Music:** Cinematic documentary beds: soft piano, ambient pads, light pulses, 80-100 BPM, low energy in investigative sections, a lift into the solution section; music shifts per section. Present under most narration ("[music]" tags appear through the transcript). Automated tempo estimates were low-confidence (71-162 BPM across windows), so treat BPM as a recommendation.
- **Sound effects:** Sparse and soft: gentle whooshes on card slides, soft pops for particles and grid squares, a low boom or tonal hit after a shocking number, ambient city/traffic beds under footage cards.
- **Mix:** Voice at -16 LUFS integrated; music at -24 to -28 dB under voice with ducking; SFX at -20 to -24 dB. Let music rise briefly (2-3 s) on diorama reveals between narration lines.

## 10. HyperFrames build recipe
- **Canvas:** 1920x1080, 30 fps. Background `#EAECE1` on canvas scenes.
- **Layer order:** cream background -> inset hairline frame (an absolutely positioned div, `inset: 32px; border: 1.5px solid #C9CAC1`, with 12 px corner bracket SVGs) -> imagery cards / diorama layers -> thin-line diagrams -> labels and leader lines -> watermark.
- **Components to build once:**
  - `media-card(src, w)`: image or video in a 3 px radius box; entrance `x: +60, opacity 0 -> 0, 1`, 0.5 s `power3.out`.
  - `outline-pie(value)`: SVG circle stroke 1.5 px, wedge as a path whose end angle tweens (use a GSAP onUpdate to rebuild the arc), value label in blue.
  - `square-grid(n, filled)`: CSS grid of 28 px hollow squares, fill sequence via stagger.
  - `measure-bracket(height, label)`: SVG path drawn with dashoffset, italic label.
  - `leader-label(point, text)`: dot + line + caps label.
  - `particle-circle(count, colours)`: random-packed dots inside a circle (precompute positions, no runtime randomness so renders are deterministic).
  - `diorama(layers[])`: stacked absolutely positioned PNG layers with per-layer parallax factor, one timeline driving scale and x.
  - `scale-bar(stops, cards)`: gradient bar + stepping cards.
- **Registry blocks:** `paper-collage-reveal` (diorama layering start), `parallax-zoom` / `cinematic-zoom` (push-ins and map zooms), `world-map` (maps), `browser-device-stage` or `scroll-feed` (read-along pages), `count-up` (values), `data-chart` (restyle to hairline grey), `grain-overlay` off (this style is clean).
- **Host footage:** full-frame video layer with a gentle warm grade (`/media-use` colour grade or a LUT: lift shadows towards `#372824`, warm highlights).
- **Pitfalls:** keep it calm: no shakes, no glitch, no bold colour blocks; one accent per scene; leave 30-40% of the canvas empty. Avoid drop shadows on cards. Dioramas made from cut-outs need consistent lighting; grade every layer to the same warm, slightly desaturated look.

## 11. Storyboard frame spec
Each frame must specify:
- `world`: host / canvas-cream / canvas-dark / web-page / map / footage-card
- `host_line` or faceless substitute
- `panel_title` (sentence case, 2 short lines) and `frame_inset` yes/no
- `cards`: list of media with sources and arrangement (single, two-up, stepped row)
- `diagram`: pie / square-grid / bracket / particles / scale-bar / diorama, with values
- `labels`: leader-line labels and positions
- `highlight`: text to select on a web page
- `camera`: static / push-in % / pan direction
- `sfx` and `music_cue` (lift, drop)
- `duration`

## 12. Do and don't
**Do**
- Open with a shocking Indian statistic, said by a person, then show its source.
- Put every piece of evidence on the cream canvas with lots of empty space.
- Use thin outlines and fill only the part that matters.
- Make abstract geography physical with a diorama and leader-line labels.
- Compare India with a country that solved the problem.
- Keep text minimal: short titles, caps labels, italic measurements.

**Don't**
- Don't burn in subtitles or kinetic captions.
- Don't use bold flat colour backgrounds, gradients or neon; the canvas is cream or dark slate.
- Don't use cartoon characters or icons; imagery is photographic or diagrammatic.
- Don't stack more than three cards in one frame.
- Don't animate aggressively (no bounces beyond a gentle `back.out(1.4)`, no shakes).
- Don't drop sources; show the real page.

## 13. Feasibility with our stack
- **Reproduces well (85%):** the cream canvas, hairline frames, outline pies, square grids, measurement brackets, particle circles, read-along web pages, card layouts and the calm motion. This is the larger share of screen time and the part that says "Aevy".
- **Hard (downgraded from medium on re-check): dioramas.** The channel's terrain cubes and city bowls are 3D renders with continuous camera flights (crane, sweep, orbit) and animated fills (smog filling the bowl). Layered PNG cut-outs with parallax give a 2.5D push, not a flight: about 50-60% fidelity. A Three.js terrain from public-domain SRTM elevation data gets real flights but is a bigger build. AI image or video generation only if the user agrees.
- **Medium: the transforms.** Strata becoming ground, the city sinking into heat lines, day turning to night (section 4b) are layered HTML/SVG with GSAP and feasible, but each needs its pieces designed to hand over to the next scene. Plan them in the storyboard, not at build time.
- **Hard: the host.** The emotional host in the warm study is the channel's anchor (about a third of runtime).
  1. **Faceless variant (free):** carry the host's lines in narration over moving people footage (Pexels/Pixabay video of the real situation) or over a diorama flight; optionally a posed Open Peeps host if the user accepts illustration (section 4c). Do not use static statement cards: the original has no text-only scenes and they read as dead air. Fidelity about 65%.
  2. **Self-filmed:** the user records host lines at a desk with a warm lamp and a window behind (golden hour or a lilac LED), medium shot, 1080p. Fidelity about 85-90%.
  3. **AI avatar:** HeyGen avatar video via `/media-use` (free allowance for new or signed-in users, then paid). It can stand in for the host but the set and the emotion are hard to match; keep shots under 6 s.
- **Hard: real news footage.** Broadcast clips are copyrighted; use free stock and public-domain imagery, or show the news article page instead.
- **Voice:** `af_heart` matches the warmth and pace; no Indian accent.
- **Overall fidelity:** about 65% faceless (lowered from 70-75%: the motion is denser and the dioramas more 3D than the first audit assumed), about 80% with a self-filmed host.
